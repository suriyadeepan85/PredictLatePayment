## What a skeptical judge would challenge

1. **"Your reader is a regex you wrote for your own templates."** Recall 1.000 and reasons only on real disputes. Until Haiku runs on paraphrased notes, the curve is an upper bound on reading (S2-02).
2. **"Count the notes and you get most of it."** A no-text model gets about 80% of the gain (S2-03). The deck should show this ablation, or the generator should remove the difference.
3. **"The curve is an assumption plotted against itself."** The x-axis (share noted) is set by you, and the y-axis follows almost mechanically from revealing the true flag. Say that the curve is a sensitivity analysis of the dispute flag's timing, anchored by a real fact: IBM disputes are late 44% to 66% of the time (D-069).
4. **"Which number is it, 14% or 40%?"** (S2-01). Also, the 70% point is noisier than its gain, and 80% scores below 70% (S2-05).
5. **"Why does every model score 0.53 in fold 1?"** (S2-04). Present folds 2 to 4, where the levels are 0.79 / 0.82 / 0.87.
6. **"Your fairness check passes a group with 2.7 times the false alarms."** (S2-08).
7. **Small data:** 1,345 training invoices, 100 customers, 5 country groups of 39 to 67 in the last fold. Every gain needs an interval.
